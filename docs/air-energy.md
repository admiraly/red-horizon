# Aircraft cruise energy

`air_energy_step` is an authored NASM/SSE2 pure SysV actuator. Inputs are role0/1, engine-powered0/1, desired/current total speed, last completed bank and vertical displacement. It preserves nonvolatile registers and stack, rejects malformed scalars and returns a new total speed and its actual signed change. No pointers, allocations, private pools or authority writes are involved.

The existing longitudinal controller supplies net level-flight acceleration or braking. Powered level flight at the requested speed has zero net work. Unpowered flight suppresses throttle and includes coast drag. Additional coordinated-turn drag is proportional to `tan(bank)^2`, equivalently normal-load-squared minus one. Gravity exchanges kinetic and potential energy using the last completed physical vertical step:

`next_speed_squared = throttle_speed_squared - 2*previous_speed*extra_drag - 2*gravity*previous_vertical_step`.

Role coefficients are tactical approximations in fixed30Hz units, not certified aircraft performance. Both actual flight and the uninterrupted final-path forecast apply the same lagged physical state and operation order before bank/vertical actuation. Published horizontal velocity is still reduced by vertical motion, and failed actual endpoints restore total speed together with the original pose. Empty engines can recover speed by descent, but receive no powered acceleration.

Existing5..7m/tick cruise and role acceleration/braking limits remain explicit safeguards. Their saturation discards residual work; minimum-speed saturation does **not** establish stall-safe flight or energy conservation at that limit. A bomber already at minimum can remain there while turning/climbing. True lower-speed flight, stall loss of lift, engine/throttle state, safe landing contact, rollout, finite service and takeoff remain dependencies. This step supplies measurable energy costs in the unsaturated current domain rather than claiming those lifecycle features.
